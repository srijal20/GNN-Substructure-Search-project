1. Title of the Project

GNN Molecular Substructure Search: Accuracy vs. Speed Trade-offs Against Classical Fingerprint Screening

2. Updates on Problem Statement (Two-Stage Cascaded Screening)

In large chemical databases (50,000+ molecules), standalone neural classification cannot guarantee zero false negatives. We have formally adapted our research formulation into a two-stage cascaded screening architecture: (1) A learned order-embedding GNN (zQ ≤ zT) rapidly screens the whole database to shortlist candidate molecules at a strict high-recall threshold (≥98%), and (2) RDKit's exact matcher (HasSubstructMatch) verifies only the shortlist, yielding 100% precision. Our empirical focus is quantifying the speedup gained over brute force exact matching while benchmarking directly against the industrial baseline: RDKit's 2048-bit Chem.PatternFingerprint screen followed by exact verification.

3. Difficulties Encountered & Technical Insights
  • Shortlist Size & Verification Bottleneck: While GNN B's order-embedding vector check is extremely fast on GPU (2.79 ms over 5,000 molecules), its lower precision at the ≥98% recall point returns an average of 1,414 candidates (vs. only 703 for Pattern Fingerprints). Because RDKit verification takes ~0.05 ms/mol, verifying those extra false positives increases overall cascaded latency to 20.2 ms (compared to 5.6 ms for Pattern FP + RDKit). Tighter precision calibration is essential to keep verification overhead from dominating.
  • Query Size Degradation (4 vs. 12 Atoms): Empirical breakdown shows strong recall (>83%) on small 4–5 atom subgraphs, but recall drops to 28%–44% on larger 9–12 atom queries. The 4-layer GINE receptive field and global sum-pooling struggle to preserve strict subgraph isomorphism as molecular diameter and cycle complexity expand.
  • Hard Negative Mining Bottleneck: Training order embeddings requires hard negatives (pairs passing fingerprint screens but failing exact matching). Generating these requires intensive RDKit exact calls during batch construction, causing CPU-side data preprocessing bottlenecks when scaling beyond 40,000 pairs.
